# GPU port (P2.7)

The GPU port: the hardware it runs on, the toolchain, the kernels' design, the phases and their gates. The history before 2026-11-15 (the Burn prototype, the sizing) is in `performance.md` P2.7 and `correctness-bugs.md` P0.11.

## Decisions (2026-11-15; the GPU may lead since 2026-11-25)

- **The GPU may lead (decided 2026-11-25; "The GPU may lead" below).** Until then every kernel was gated bit for bit against the CPU, which fixed the kernels to the CPU's loops and summation order, all 36 node pairs at P2, its divisions, and host round trips wherever the host's own function was the only way to its bits. Now the GPU's numerics may differ from the CPU's where that pays: other summation orders, atomics, other loop shapes, multiply–add contraction, another form of the same scheme, a scheme of its own. What holds it: (1) its own judges, `dgcom-gpu judges` (convergence, rest, conservation, positivity; 2D and 3D), which ask the scheme's properties of the device and nothing of the CPU; (2) the gates against the CPU, bit for bit where a kernel still mirrors the CPU in a build without contraction, within a stated tolerance otherwise, each with mutants it must catch; (3) the scored runs, within their noise. Every place where the backends differ is in the table of that section. Runs stay reproducible: the same binary on the same card gives the same bits. Reduced or emulated precision is an experiment behind a switch, off by default.
- **CUDA first**, on the cards we have: an RTX 3090 (local) and an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (work). The production card is open: a CUDA card with full-rate FP64 (H100/H200/GH200, B200) or AMD's MI300X. Neither the B300 (≈ 1.3 TF FP64) nor the RTX PRO 6000 (≈ 1.9 TF) is a production FP64 card.
- **Kernels in Rust, with NVIDIA's cuda-oxide** (the SIMT track of "CUDA Rust", announced 2026-09-08; we use 0.4, git `348895e`, 2026-11-13). It compiles ordinary Rust to PTX: structs, enums, generics, closures and device functions from other crates, core-only (no `std`, no heap), f64 with libdevice's math (`sqrt`, `cbrt`, …), shared memory, f64 atomics and shuffles, launch contracts. So the GPU kernels call **the same pointwise physics as the CPU kernels** (fluxes, reconstruction, friction, sources), checked bit for bit (spike below).
- **One source for the physics (decided 2026-11-15, see the results below).** The 8-wide SIMD batch that real runs use (`simd`, on by default) had its own copies of the pointwise physics. It is written once now, generic over the lane type (`crate::types::Lane`: `f64`, or `f64x8` under `simd`): the scalar kernel and the GPU instantiate it with `f64`, the batch with `f64x8`.
- Not Burn (tensor ops; f64 fusion off, ≈ 13× the bytes per node-stage; P0.11), not cutile-rs (tile model: our kernels gather across faces and branch on wet/dry; f64 not documented), not CubeCL (its `#[cube]` DSL cannot call ordinary Rust, so the physics would exist twice; API breaks each release), not CUDA C++ through NVRTC (a second language).
- **Risk:** cuda-oxide is alpha (0.1 in May 2026, 0.4 now; API breaks expected), and its toolchain pin moves with its releases. Pin its git revision; keep the shared physics plain Rust with no cuda-oxide dependency (it ports to any later backend, MI300X's included); gate every kernel bit for bit against the CPU.

- The 2D stage needs an estimated 300–500 FP64 operations and ≈ 126 B per node and stage (`review-2026-09-25.md` §5.2): ≈ 3 flop/byte. **On the 1:64 cards it is FP64-bound**, on the data-centre cards bandwidth-bound. On the 1:64 cards load what is static (geometry, 1/J, normals, friction factors) rather than recompute it, compute 1/h once per node, and spare divisions, square and cube roots (multi-instruction sequences on the scarce FP64 units).
- The 3090's FP64 peak is about the laptop CPU's. What the GPU wins is running near its peak, where the CPU code runs far below its own (gathers, branches).
- **Predictions** (Phase 0 checks them; the FLOP count of step 1 sharpens them): a fully wet P2 element-stage takes 10–30 ns on the 3090 and 3–9 ns on the PRO 6000, against 71 ns on the laptop's 24 threads (Chiloé 2D, whole stage). A Chiloé model hour: 102.5 s on the laptop, 10–30 s on the 3090, 4–10 s on the PRO 6000.
- Small meshes do not fill a GPU (Kattholmen's 3,584 elements, `performance.md` P2.7 sizing): the GPU is for meshes of 10⁴–10⁵ elements and up, and for several runs at once.

## Spike: dgcom's own flux on the GPU (2026-11-15)

A cuda-oxide kernel calling `dgcom::flux::wintermeyer_flux_2d` and `entropy_stable_dissipation_2d` unchanged (dgcom as a path dependency, `--no-default-features --features parallel`, built by the pinned toolchain in about a minute), one thread per face node, 2²⁰ random node pairs: dry, below `h_min`, films and 0–200 m deep, ±2 m/s, beds to −50 m.

- Default build (FMA contraction): 30 % bit-identical to the CPU, the rest within 1.1e-12 of max(|F|, 1).
- `--no-fmad`: **1,048,576 of 1,048,576 bit-identical.**
- So the GPU's gates can be bit for bit against the CPU (a `--no-fmad` build); production builds keep FMA and tolerance gates.

## Architecture

- **One source for the physics.** The pointwise functions are generic over the lane type (`Lane`: arithmetic, `sqrt`, compare-and-select, `max`/`min`, with `f64` and `f64x8` implementations), and the scalar kernel, the batch and the GPU instantiate them. Branches are selects, as the batch wrote them, which on a GPU also avoids divergent warps; `Lane::any` keeps the expensive arms (the wall pressure) for the lanes that need them. What stays per target is what an element reads and the loops around the physics: the batch derives its geometry from the corners (64 B against ≈ 1 kB at P2, P2.8), the GPU on the 1:64 cards loads it.
- **Crates.** The pointwise physics (node states, interface fluxes, the subcells' reconstruction, the wall ram pressure, friction rates, Coriolis and the other sources) moves to a small crate of `#[inline]` functions on `Copy` structs that both the CPU kernels and the GPU kernels call. The kernels' crate (cuda-oxide) and the host driver (a `cuda` feature, replacing `burn*`/`gpu`) build on it.
- **Host side unchanged.** `Simulation<S, P, I>` is generic over the state: a device-resident `SWESolution2D` (SoA as on the CPU, `[var][k·n_nodes + i]`) implements `Integrable` (scale, axpy, combine, is_finite as kernels), a `GpuSWEPhysics2D` built from the same `SWEPhysics2D` implements `PhysicsModule` and `LocalTimeStepping`, and the runner, `SSPRK43` and `Multirate` drive it from the host. A case picks it with `[compute] device = "cuda:0"`.
- **A stage** is a face kernel (each face once, as `SubsetEdges::owns` on the CPU, into a face buffer) and an element kernel (volume term: flux differencing, or the subcells for an element with a dry node, sorted so a warp does not mix them; lifting the face buffer; sources; the Shu–Osher combination; implicit friction; wet/dry correction; positivity limiter). No atomics: deterministic and conservative to round-off, as the CPU. Alternative, one kernel per pass: each element computes its own faces' fluxes with the lower-numbered element as the minus side, so both sides compute the same bits; worth it where launches dominate (the data-centre cards).
- **Local time stepping** (done 2026-11-16, below). Levels and rates are assigned on the host and go up once per coarse step (`element_dt` comes down); a pass's plan is data (`StagePlan`), each element's stage the shared `element_stage`. A Chiloé coarse step is ≈ 1,280 passes (2⁸ finest substeps × 5), ≈ 5,000 launches at 4 a pass: measure the overhead there; CUDA graphs per coarse step if it passes 10 %; fewer levels may pay on a GPU than on the CPU.
- **Forcing.** Boundary conditions, external-state providers and sources are trait objects running arbitrary Rust per node; the device needs a closed set. A `device_form()` returning plain data (a run with a term without one refuses the GPU and names it); done for walls, `CharacteristicOBC` over a uniform external state, Coriolis and Manning (2026-11-15, below), atlas tides per boundary node and nesting with its relaxation band (2026-11-16, below). The case runner's set: walls; `CharacteristicOBC` over `StillWater`, `HarmonicTide` and `BoundaryTides` (harmonic constants per boundary node, evaluated on the device), `OceanModelState` (parent snapshots time-interpolated on the device, uploaded when the parent advances), `InverseBarometer`; Coriolis (f- and β-plane); Manning/Chézy and `CageDrag2D` (implicit); `GriddedAtmosphere2D` (interpolation weights precomputed, two snapshots); `RiverSources`; the nesting relaxation; `HorizontalViscosity2D` (BR1, the largest kernel, Kattholmen's only: last).
- **Host–device traffic** only at outputs (stations, snapshots), when forcing snapshots change, and once per coarse step.

## Phase 0 results (2026-11-15)

- **Burn deleted** (P0.11 closed): 2,233 lines, the `burn*`/`gpu` features and their dependency tree (1,983 lines of `Cargo.lock`), `MeshGPUData`.
- **`gpu/`**, a workspace of its own on cuda-oxide's toolchain (as `viz/`): `edge_fluxes` (a thread per interior edge, both sides' F* into a face buffer, as `SplitFormSWE2D::edge_fluxes`) and `element_rhs` (a thread per element: the flux-differencing volume term with the bed, the lifted face terms, 1/J, as `element_rhs` without subcells), P2, on doubly periodic meshes of 100 m quadrilaterals (parallelograms, and interior vertices jittered by 0.15 of the size: no element affine), a smooth bed 12–28 m deep and a smooth flow. The kernels call `SWENodeState2D::new`, `wintermeyer_flux_2d`, `entropy_stable_dissipation_2d`, `wintermeyer_bed_interface_term_2d` and `SWEState2D`'s arithmetic from dgcom unchanged, and mirror the loops around them.
- **The gate holds:** under `--no-fmad` every node of the RHS is the CPU's (`compute_rhs_swe_2d_parallel_into`) bit for bit, against both CPU copies of the physics: the scalar kernel (`gpu/` without its `simd` feature) and the 8-wide batch that real runs use (`--features simd`). 17,280 nodes on 1,920 elements and 864,000 on 96,000, parallelograms and jittered. With FMA contraction: within 1.3e-12 (small meshes) and 1.2e-11 (large) of each field's largest value.
- **Time per element evaluation of the RHS**, 96,000 P2 elements, RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (FP64 at 1:64, ≈ 0.2 TF) against the HX 370's 24 threads (20 CPU, 100 GPU evaluations; the CPU varies ±5 % between runs):

  | | CPU | GPU, `--no-fmad` | GPU, FMA |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | jittered | 49–54 ns | 31.5 ns | 24.9–25.7 ns |
  | parallelograms | 45–50 ns | 22.7 ns | 17.7 ns |

  The laptop's own GPU is 2–2.8× its 24 threads on this term. (The CPU column is the batch, `--features simd`; the scalar kernel takes 73 ns jittered and 54 ns on parallelograms: the batch's geometry from the corners pays on curved elements.)
- **Registers, not local memory.** The first version kept the element's nodes and RHS in local memory (130 loads and 173 stores in the PTX): its loops did not unroll, and the face nodes came from a table. `#[unroll]` on every loop (on `#[device]` helpers, with constant trip counts: `(a + 1)..N1` as `0..N1` with `continue`), the face nodes as a formula (`face_node`, checked against `ops.face_nodes` on the host) and the face buffer read per access instead of copied: no local memory left, 35.8 → 31.5 ns (jittered, `--no-fmad`), the same bits.
- **FP64-bound, by a rough count:** ≈ 3,100 FP64 instructions per element evaluation in the PTX (both kernels, the non-affine path, divisions expanded) at 31.5 ns is ≈ 100 G per second, about the 4060 Laptop's FP64 peak. If so the time scales with the card's FP64 rate: ≈ 10 ns (jittered) and 7 (parallelograms) on the 3090, ≈ 3 and 2 on the PRO 6000, with FMA; inside the predictions (10–30 and 3–9 for the whole stage). Nsight Compute counts the instructions exactly once the GPU's performance counters are open to the user (NVIDIA Control Panel → Developer → Manage GPU Performance Counters → all users; or an administrator prompt): `ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM` without.
- **Left of Phase 0:** the timing on the 3090 and the PRO 6000 (`cargo oxide run` in `gpu/`), the Nsight counts, and the stage combination (with Phase 1's device state).

## Phase 1: `Simulation` on the GPU (2026-11-15)

- **Where the backend lives:** in `gpu/`, which depends on dgcom; dgcom stays free of CUDA. `swe2d.rs` holds the kernels, `DeviceState` (dgcom's `Integrable`: `combine` as two kernels, with a base and in place, in `combine_values`' order; `copy_from` device to device; `is_finite` a per-element flag, reduced on the host) and `GpuSWE2D` (dgcom's `PhysicsModule`: `compute_rhs_into` the edge and element kernels, `compute_dt` a per-element rate kernel and a max on the host, which is exact in any order). dgcom's `Simulation` and its SSP-RK steppers drive them unchanged. dgcom must keep building on cuda-oxide's pinned toolchain for this; a case file choosing the GPU (`[compute] device`) needs a hook in the case runner that `gpu/` fills (Phase 2), since dgcom cannot depend on `gpu/`.
- **One source for the time step too:** `node_reference_rate` and `dt_from_reference_rate` are public pointwise functions (with `g` and `h_min` in place of `&ShallowWater2D`), which the CPU's `compute_dt_swe_2d` and the GPU's rate kernel both call.
- **The gate holds:** `Simulation` with SSP-RK(4,3) at CFL 0.5, to t = 30 s on the periodic beds of Phase 0: the GPU takes the CPU's steps (50, 73 and 82: the same dt every step) and ends on the CPU's state bit for bit under `--no-fmad`, against the scalar kernel and against the batch (`SWEPhysics2D`'s fused stages), 17,280 and 864,000 nodes. With FMA: within 7e-14 of each field's largest value after 82 steps.
- **Time per step**, 96,000 P2 elements, 82 steps (FMA, the batch on the CPU): CPU 25.3 ms, RTX 4060 Laptop 11.2 ms (2.26×); per element and stage 65.8 against 29.1 ns. ## Phase 2: local time stepping on the device (2026-11-16)

- **The trait change.** `LocalTimeStepping::stage_where` takes a `StagePlan` (the coarse step's start and length, the finest substep and its depth, the stage and its Shu–Osher weights, whether it is the last, and the levels and rates) in place of `plan: &dyn Fn(usize) -> ElementStage`: `StagePlan::at(k)` and the device's stage kernel both call `time::element_stage`, so an element's stage time and weights are the same bits on either. `begin_coarse_step(level, rates)` (default no-op) hands a device the coarse step's levels once; `finish_where` gets the pass's depth. The mode splitter's barotropic pass and the 3D stages call `plan.at(k)`. `SWEDeviceForm2D` carries the line-local depth and the positivity bound's `h_dry` from the CPU's own functions; the case runner hands a device its levels (`DeviceRun::levels`) instead of refusing them.
- **On the device** (`GpuSWE2D` is dgcom's `LocalTimeStepping`): every pass's elements are those whose key (the stage's rate, or the level for a finish) is at least the pass's depth, so each key's elements sorted largest first make every pass a prefix: `begin_coarse_step` sorts them (and per stage the interior and boundary edges by the larger key of their two elements) on the host and uploads them when the levels change; a pass launches over the first n. The stage kernel (`stage_elements`) does what `SWEPhysics2D::stage_where`'s closure does per element: `element_stage` from its level (and, the last stage, its rate), the combination, `damp_node`, the line-local check, then the sum into the substep's result or the wet/dry correction; `finish_elements` the end of a substep. Open boundaries: within a pass the elements of a level share a time, so the host evaluates the uniform external state per level (at most 9: the device takes 8 levels) and uploads them per stage; a boundary edge reads its element's level's. With one level (global SSP steps) the fused stages run on every element, no lists: `Simulation` takes them for global steps now too, as on the CPU.
- **Gates, bit for bit** (`--no-fmad`, against the SIMD batch): `cases/tidal_channel` with 3 levels (1,470 coarse steps of 31–64 s against 5,765 global ones; 91 % of the elements line-local, none broke it) and 8 levels (the channel uses 3), with `velocity = "incoming_wave"` (1,529 steps), and refined to 64,000 elements at 8 levels (143 coarse steps of 6.29 s, 2³ finest substeps): steps, volume, clips and every station record. The global gates (`all`, `beach`, `case`) stay bit for bit through the fused stages. `gpu/time.sh` runs the 3-level case gate (`case_lts`).
- **Two costs found on the way.** (1) cuda-oxide copies a by-value aggregate kernel parameter into every thread's local memory at entry: the per-level boundary data (1 kB) as a parameter put 135 local loads and stores in every edge thread (`edge_fluxes_hll` 1.69 → 2.08 ms per step). Now it is a device buffer (copied per stage from a ring of host slots), and the boundary edges have a kernel of their own, so the interior edge kernels have no local memory. (2) The boundary kernel, one thread per edge, is latency-bound: 1,760 edges are 55 warps on 82 SMs, each a long FP64 sequence at 1:64 (0.31 ms per step); one thread per face node: 0.12 ms. Global steps at 64,000 elements: 8.4 s for the 15 min (7.3 ms per step, kernels 6.92 ms), against 8.5 s before the change.

## Phase 4.4a: the vertical step on the device (2026-11-23)

- **Elementary functions with the same bits everywhere (`dgcom::math`).** The vertical step is the first device code with transcendentals: GLS raises to non-integer powers throughout, the log-layer drag takes a logarithm, the shortwave's absorption two exponentials, the friction velocities `hypot`. Measured on 1 M pairs (`x` 1e-8–1e2, `y` −3–3): device against CPU, 0 differences in each function; each within 1 ulp of MSVC's on 20,000 arguments per function over decades (unit test). ## Phase 4.5d: the 3D stages on local time steps on the device (2026-11-24)

- **Why.** P4.5 weighs local time stepping of the 3D stages (`[three_d] levels_lts`) as a default, and the device refused it by name (`Cuda::run_3d`; `Model3D`'s `start_turbulence`, `accumulate_stage`, `substep_values`, `restart_substep`). On local steps the splitter hands every section of a pass a list of elements at their own stage times (`StageTime::PerElement`): a stage's elements whose rate reaches the finest substep's depth, the elements whose substeps end there, a level's elements at a substep's end. It keeps every stage's inventories and values in slots of their own and sums the substeps' last stages (`acc`), each element with its own Shu–Osher weights (`StagePlan::at`).
- **The lists** (`gpu/src/listed.rs`): `Lists` keeps a pass's elements on the device by content (a coarse step's lists come pass after pass, and step after step while the levels hold; up to 96 kept, the least recently used dropped), in two forms. `listed`, the elements, for a kernel with a thread per value of the listed elements (`listed_index`). `member`, a flag per element, for a kernel over another collection: the elements with a merged group, the flushed elements' waves, the columns with an implicit part. An empty buffer is every element (`On::ALL`), so the global path's launches are the same.
- **What acts on the listed elements alone**, everything a pass writes into the stage buffers that outlive it (`gpu/src/model3d.rs`):
  - the combination and the substeps' sums (`combine`, `accumulate`), every element's weights from dgcom's own `element_stage` of its level and rate (`StagePlanDevice`; `Levels3D` uploads the levels and the stage's rates as the plan's change);
  - the implicit vertical advection;
  - a stage's values, from another slot's last values and stash, with the Kuzmin limiter's limiting and bounds (its pre-passes over every element: a patch reads its elements' current values, as the CPU's does), the density, the inventories, the dry nodes and the mask;
  - the substeps' results (`finish_substep`), and a substep's end: its values (`substep_values`), its vertical step on that values slot with the surface fluxes over the slot's own surface, the depth means, the limiter and the state's inventories (`restart_substep`);
  - the vertical step of each level's elements at the step's end.
- **A stage's RHS.** The kernels that write only their own element's tendencies take the pass's list: the horizontal and vertical advection of the layers and the w-points, the σ-pairs pressure gradient with Coriolis, the reference's advection (edges, totals, correction) and the viscosity's diffusion. ## The GPU may lead: the gates' bars and the device's own judges (2026-11-25)

## Local 3D steps: what the judges and the long runs found (2026-11-25)

Found while timing the stack; none of it is the GPU's alone. The scheme is dgcom's (`ModeSplitIntegrator` with `baroclinic_levels`, `MultirateSSPRK3`), the same on both backends. `TODO.md` P4.5 has the line.

- **A uniform tracer does not stay uniform** (found 2026-11-26 and fixed: the turbulence closure and the vertical diffusion's round-off, below). On the judges' wet/dry beach under its sheared flow and wind (720 P2 elements × 20 levels, films and thin columns, 40 steps, two levels in use), uniform T and S drift by 2.65e-6, and by 4.31e-6 with three rivers of the same water. Global steps keep them to 1.4e-8 on the same run. The CPU's kernels give 2.65e-6 and 4.31e-6 too, and 2.59e-6 with every element kept on the level of its first step, so it is not the levels' changing. The inventories hold (8.1e-12). dgcom's own test (`beach_tracers_are_constant_and_conserved_with_local_time_steps`) asks constancy of its beach with levels fixed by position and finds 5e-8; what differs here is not found (the start's thin columns carrying their depth means, the films, the end of step). `dgcom-gpu judges` prints the numbers on every run.
- **Frøya's 71 h on 3 levels, a 240 s cap and CFL 4 blows up**, in `T`, on `main` as on the stack: `main` (04a67a2) at 35.66 h (78 elements, the first 4620 at (−8624, 743) m), the stack at 46.40 h (249 elements, the first 5339 at (−8624, 3716) m), each run reproducibly. From the stack's restart at 46 h both binaries blow up at 46.40 h. From the same restart, global steps pass (their step falls to 18.6 s in that hour: an event), and so do 3 levels with a 120 s cap and 3 levels at CFL 3 (the coarse step falls to 115 s). The run of 2026-11-24 at 71a2be9 (`output/p45_gpu/lts3_240`, before 4.5k and 4.5l) went through all 71 h with its coarse step down to 92 s: another trajectory. So the P4.5 candidate \"3 levels, 240 s\" is not stable through that event; which last digits a run carries decides whether and when.
- **The coastline mesh's local steps.** Its elements' steps span 3 to 60 s under the tide (1 % under 12 s, the median 61 s, down to 1.7 s within the first hour). On 4 levels the coarse step follows the smallest element times eight and an hour costs what global steps cost. On 6 levels `main`'s binary completes the first hour (61 coarse steps of 53–60 s) and the stack's blows up at 0.87 h (T, 171 elements, the first 2015 at (−2471, −9565) m; river 851 enters element 2071). Three builds with one change each put it on the nesting band's sampling: with the general kernel the stack completes the hour, with the CPU's element steps it still blows up. The two sampling kernels agree within 1e-9 of a field's largest value at all 1,712 evaluations of that hour. A difference in the last digits of the band decides the blow-up: the run is on the edge on 6 levels, with either binary.
- **First cuts at the drift** (2026-11-25, after the merge; the CPU's kernels alone, the judges' fixtures, 40 coarse steps of two levels, ≈ 2,100 s; the scripts and logs are in `output/radical/drift/`, each a block put before the judges' tracers under `DGCOM_DRIFT_VARIANTS`):
  - **It comes in single coarse steps, not as a slow growth.** On the beach under a uniform stress of 0.03 N/m² the largest deviation goes from 3.9e-8 to 2.4e-5 in the second coarse step (45.5 s), in element 589, layer 6: 4.0–4.4 m of water that fell to 3.6–4.0 m in that step, the element and its four neighbours all on the finest level (their own steps 15–21 s). At 0.12 N/m²: 1.3e-7 → 2.8e-5 at step 29 (element 437, its own step 41 s between neighbours of 28 and 47 s) and → 1.2e-4 at step 34 (element 551). The elements are wet, 4–7 m deep, away from the shore.
  - **Its size follows no input smoothly.** Uniform stress of 0.03, 0.04, 0.05, 0.06, 0.08, 0.12 N/m² on the beach: 2.4e-5, 3.4e-8, 2.1e-8, 1.2e-8, 4.3e-6, 1.2e-4 (T alone). With [0.03, −0.01] N/m², the judge's own: 7.2e-9. Whether an event falls inside the 40 steps decides.
  - **Not needed for it:** rivers, the levels' changing between coarse steps (2.59e-6 with them kept), the barotropic pass's own levels (4.4e-6 with the pass on global substeps), the reference profile (8.0e-7 without), cages (1.3e-6), implicit vertical advection (3.7e-5 with it explicit), the shear's viscosity (1.9e-3 without: less mixing, more drift), a stress field's variation in time or space (a uniform constant one does it).
  - **The shore may be needed.** On the all-wet periodic bumps a uniform stress of 0.03 to 0.12 N/m² keeps 4e-11 to 2.5e-8, though the fixture's turning field gives 1.05e-6 there (and only 8 % of its evaluations are saved: few elements on a finer level).
  - **Vertical mixing hides most of it.** With no mixing (k-ε at a floor, no background) the same run reaches 6 in T's units after 1,254 s with the wind field on: the tracer is then far from conserved pointwise, and that run's currents reach 6 m/s in the top layer, so it is not a fair fixture, but it says the error is made by advection and diffused after.
  - **dgcom's own beach does not show it**: ten elements in a row, constant mixing, levels fixed by position, under a stress field constant or varying in space and time: 5e-12 to 8e-12 (a temporary test beside `beach_tracers_are_constant_and_conserved_with_local_time_steps`). A 2D mesh, or the fast change of depth, is what that fixture lacks.
- **Found (2026-11-26, branch `fix/local-steps-drift`): not the layer volumes but the turbulence, through the vertical diffusion's round-off.** A probe after every operation of the 3D stages (the largest `|T − 12.3|` of `q/H_z` and of the values, on the elements of each pass; `output/radical/drift/probes_and_experiments.patch`, `DGCOM_DRIFT_PROBE`) on the 0.03 N/m² event: every stage's inventories and values stay uniform to 3e-8 through the second coarse step, the transport, the combination, the implicit vertical advection and the conversions included. The jump is in the implicit vertical terms at the end of its first fine substep: uniform to 6e-10 before, 3.7e-5 after, in element 590 (4.27 m deep). There `K_t` was 2.1·10⁷ m²/s mid-column, from `k` of 4,300 m²/s², and at `K Δt/(Δz Δz_w)` ≈ 10⁹ the column solve for the new value loses the `1` of its diagonal to round-off: a uniform tracer comes out 1e-5 off, uniformly over the well-mixed block.
  - **Why `K_t` was 10⁷:** after a substep's advection, ψ was negative over the upper column and k 0.7–1.7 m²/s² beside ψ of 10⁻⁶ at a few w-points: lengths `c k^{3/2}/ε` of 4·10⁴ m, which nothing limits in unstratified water (Galperin's limit acts for N² > 0 only; the beach's uniform T and S make it unstratified). The closure's explicit production `ν S²` with that `ν` made `k` of thousands within the step. The same degeneracy is in global steps, smaller: `K_t` up to 218 m²/s on the beach, and on Frøya from its 46 h restart up to 5.7·10⁵ m²/s with `k` of 2.3·10⁵ m²/s² (54 columns over 1 m²/s per step), 4.1·10⁷ on 3 levels of local steps (`notes/3d-mixing.md`).
  - **Why local steps had it worse:** not the level interfaces. With every element but one far away on the finest level (15 s substeps in a 60 s coarse step) element 590's turbulence degenerates as on the original levels (`k²/ε` 3·10⁴ at the step's end); on global 15 s steps it stays under 0.2. The substeps inside one coarse step see the pass's η and ū move at constant rates over 60 s, a steeper fall at first than the 15 s passes follow (element 590 at 15 s: η 0.11 against 0.25 m, ū −0.50 against −0.36 m/s), and the turbulence's advection undershoots more (38 of the element's 171 interior w-points not positive against 14). The w-cell transport keeps a uniform `k` and `ψ` uniform on both (8e-12 global, 6e-7 at a dry node on substeps): it is not a constancy defect of the turbulence's transport.
  - **The first cuts' readings, corrected:** "vertical mixing hides most of it" and "made by advection and diffused after" were wrong. Without the shear's viscosity the run's turbulence degenerated further; with no mixing at all the run itself degenerated.
  - **The fix, two parts:** (1) the vertical diffusion solves for the increment `δ = φⁿ⁺¹ − φⁿ` from the residual in flux form (`vertical_diffusion::solve_diffusion_pair`, its module docs): a uniform field with no flux or drag has a zero residual and stays uniform bit for bit at any `K` (gate `a_uniform_column_stays_uniform_under_any_diffusivity`, which the old solve fails at 1e-3 m²/s already); alone, the beach's 0.03 N/m² event goes from 2.4e-5 to 1.4e-9 (what is left is at dry nodes). (2) GLS's length at most the distance to the nearer boundary (`GlsMixing::wall_length`; `notes/3d-mixing.md`): `K_t` on Frøya at most 103 m²/s, global and local alike.
  - **The judges since:** uniform T and S drift by 8.4e-9 on local steps with rivers (7.0e-9 without), the CPU's kernels 7.4e-9; global steps 7.4e-9. `dgcom-gpu judges` holds local steps to global steps' bar (5e-8) with the same mutant (the rivers 1e-3 °C warmer: 1.4e-3, caught): 17 judges, 14 mutants.
- **Frøya's 46 h event is not the drift.** Neither fix moves the GPU's blow-up from the 46 h restart on 3 levels at a 240 s cap: with the increment alone T is not finite at 46.40 h in 250 elements, with both S in 244 (element 5220 at (−8131, 3220) m, beside the original's 5339). The CPU's kernels (`dgcom run`, the same source with the increment, `restart.changes = ['compute.device']`) pass it: 9 coarse steps of 112.5–225 s to 46.5 h. The GPU stops at 167,040 s = 46 h + 6 × 240 s, so it took 240 s coarse steps where the CPU's element steps gave 225: the two backends do not take the same steps through the event. Next for that line: the device's coarse steps and finest levels beside the CPU's from the restart, then the CPU forced onto the GPU's 240 s.
- **What follows.** Local 3D steps keep uniform tracers uniform on the beach as global steps do, and the drift is understood. They are still not the default: Frøya's 240 s cap blows up on the GPU at the 46 h event (above), and the coastline mesh's 6 levels are on the edge (above). Until those are understood: a 120 s cap on Frøya's 500 m grid, global steps on the coastline mesh.
