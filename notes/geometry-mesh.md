# Geometry and meshes for real coastlines (P1.3)

### P1.3 Geometry for real coastlines
- [x] Per-node isoparametric geometric factors (`[K]` → `[K × n_nodes]`), parallelogram-only panic removed (2026-09-26). `GeometricFactors2D::compute(mesh, ops)` evaluates the bilinear map at every node: metric, J, mass weights w·J, and per face node the normal and sJ from the same contravariant vectors (`sJ n = ±J∇r, ±J∇s`), so the discrete metric identities hold to round-off.
  - Kernels: the collocated SWE, tracer, advection and BR1 diffusion volume terms are in conservative form `J⁻¹[Dr(J∇r·F) + Ds(J∇s·F)]` with the per-node lift scale sJ/J; the split forms use the curvilinear Wintermeyer et al. (2017) form (`{{Ja}}` metric averages, bed term included); the `WetDry` subcells use the telescoping interface metrics (Fisher et al. 2013; Hennemann et al. 2021) plus a metric balance term in the bed source (zero on parallelograms and flat beds). Limiter, wet/dry and diagnostic means are mass-weighted (`LimiterContext2D` and the limiter / wet-dry functions take `&GeometricFactors2D`).
  - Gates on distorted periodic meshes (`tests/curvilinear_2d_test.rs`): free stream, lake at rest over rough and shoreline beds, mass/momentum/tracer conservation, entropy; convergence N+1 for advection and the split forms, 2 for the subcells (`convergence_test.rs`, docs/accuracy.md).
  - Parallelograms take a fast path from a dense per-element copy of the node-0 geometry (`ElementGeometry`): the per-node fields are strided and cost ≈ 9 cache lines per element. Frøya (all parallelograms, `profile=80`, mains power, CPUs 0–7, five alternating runs, min/median): RHS 1 thread 4.44/4.51 ms against 4.36/4.38 on `main` (+2–3 %), 24 threads 1.22/1.26 against 1.17/1.20 (+4–5 %); `dt` 0.246/0.251 against 0.259/0.262 (−5 %); step at 24 threads +4–5 %. Before the fast paths the RHS was ≈ 10–20 % slower. [ ] Find the remaining few percent with a profiler (AMD uProf is installed).
  - [x] The 3D kernels on general quadrilaterals (2026-10-03, `tests/curvilinear_3d_test.rs`). `Hydrostatic3D::new` no longer asserts parallelograms.
    - The PGF takes the metric per node and per face node, the 3D limiters weight with `w_i J_i`, and the 3D step takes each node's length (now the narrow side of elongated elements, as in 2D: 10:1 rectangles get 1.74× shorter steps).
    - The 3D divergences take the 2D module's metric form (`MetricForm`): the split forms' mass flux is `{{J∇r}}·{{hu}}`, not the collocated `D(J∇r·hu)`. Without the match the splitter's nodal identity failed everywhere, and a distorted seamount at rest jumped to 0.9 m/s. The PGF takes the averaged metric as the advection's adjoint.
    - Gates: rest, exactness, constancy, inventories, momentum, PGF and advection convergence (see CHANGELOG).
  - [ ] `batched` SIMD and the Burn prototype still use one metric per element (`affine_metric`); `compute_volume_terms_batched` and `BurnGeometricFactors2D::from_cpu` assert parallelograms.

          - Mechanism. The split form exchanges potential energy with the σ-pairs force exactly for any ρ, and it also conserves the tracer variance `Σ H_z ρ²`. For a linear `ρ̄(z)`, energy plus `g/(2|∂_z ρ̄|)` times the variance has its minimum at rest (an energy–Casimir argument), so rest is stable. For a curved profile no conserved functional has a minimum there. The linearised exchange would need each node's weight `1/|∂_z ρ̄|` to equal `Δz/Δρ̄` for every pair the node is in. Where a σ-level crosses the pycnocline between two nodes of one element, the pair's chord is far steeper than the stratification at either node.

        - **Literature and analysis 2026-10-04: `docs/stratified-rest-over-steep-beds.md`.** In short:
          - The literature knows the instability. Beckmann & Haidvogel 1993 found z-interpolated PGFs less stable, as the constant-depth form is here. Zängl 2012: unstable when neighbours' heights differ by much more than the layer spacing. Shepherd 1993: energy alone is not enough, rest needs a Casimir (the APE). Ricardo, Duru & Lee 2024 saw the same failure in thermal shallow water.
          - Operational answers are geometric: NorKyst smooths to rx0 0.34 with Haney ≈ 6 and keeps levels near the surface; SCHISM LSC² uses z-based gradients on per-node grids; multi-envelope levels follow smoothed virtual bottoms; ROMS wet–dry masks the flux and the PGF together.
          - With pairs by level index, conservation and exact energy, a stable rest forces the pair density's linearisation, so its stiffness (≈ chord/2G: up to 65 at the slice's cliff, half its levels > 10) is unavoidable. An entropy-*stable* pair (clipped toward upwind) equals `ρ*` at Q = 0, so it is stiff too, unless discontinuous in Q (Coulomb friction ≈ 3e-3 m/s² at the cliff).
          - Anomaly diffusion can feed the APE across a straddling pair (two nodes at G = 0.25 and 4e-4 kg/m⁴: `sym(G⁻¹L)` has an eigenvalue of +516). That explains why it only slowed the growth.

### The smoothed bed no longer depends on the elements' numbering (2026-11-12)
- **Found** when the Gmsh reader began to number the elements along a Hilbert curve (`notes/performance.md`, P2.8): the same Frøya coastline mesh in the other order had another 3D bed. `smooth_element_slopes` took 3132 sweeps for 2320 and changed 31,034 nodes for 31,063, the NorKyst point's station stood in 25.7 m for 23.0 m, the water-only mesh had 12,012 elements for 12,010, and the first half hour took 67 steps for 64.
- **Cause:** `smooth_element_slopes` and `smooth_rx0` are Gauss–Seidel sweeps over node pairs, and `NodeGraph` numbered its global nodes by first appearance in the elements and sorted the pairs by those numbers. A model's bed was a function of how its mesh generator counted: a mesh written again by another Gmsh version would have moved it the same way.
- **Fix:** `NodeGraph` is the mesh's geometry only. Its global nodes are numbered by position (x, then y; a set of coincident nodes by the least of its nodes' positions, which each element maps to round-off of the others'), and a global node's mass and depth are summed over its coincident nodes in the order of the terms' values. The sweeps, the priority flood's ties (`fill_tidal_ponds`) and every report are then the same to the last bit however the elements are numbered. Gate: `test_smoothing_does_not_depend_on_the_numbering_of_the_elements` (the shoal bed against itself in Hilbert order, both smoothers, P1–P3; with the nodes numbered by element it fails by 0.2–1.3 m).
- **The other bed steps were read for the same fault and have none:** `raise_isolated_wet_nodes` (a raised node's neighbours are dry and never move again), `lower_wall_land`, `raise_cliff_shores` (one pass each), `fill_tidal_ponds` (a spill level is the least over paths). `make_continuous` sums in element order: round-off.
- **On Frøya** (`froya_real_data`, the 72 h run's command for half an hour from rest): the mesh in file order and in Hilbert order now print the same bed (3036 sweeps, 31,438 nodes changed by up to 226.1 m, the same cliff shores, ponds, 12,014 water elements and station depths). At a fixed step (`dt_3d=20`) the NorKyst point's depth-mean current of the two runs differs by 1e-8 m/s after 10 min and 1e-6 m/s (6e-5 of it) after 20 min. That is more than the sums' order explains in 60 steps of a 2D run (Chiloé: 0.1–0.4 mm of η in 25 h) and is not explained; it is four orders below what the validation reads.

### The stratified rest state about a reference profile (2026-11-20, 2026-11-21)

The P1.3 blocker (a stratified ocean at rest over steep beds spins up) has a cure in the scheme, not in the bed. Derivation, proof, the routes ruled out and where the proof stops: `docs/stratified-rest-over-steep-beds.md`, "The cure". Code: `solver::rhs::baroclinic` ("About a reference"), `ReferenceFields`, `add_reference_advection`, `Hydrostatic3D::with_reference_profile`. In short:

- **The cause, in one line.** Linearised about rest, the tracers' conservative advection makes a node's density anomaly from the *chords* of the reference along its edges (the pairs of nodes of an element on a level, the σ-surfaces of its column), and the σ-pairs force answers through the edges' *height* differences. The two are adjoint in a positive norm only if all of a node's chords have one slope: a profile linear in `z`, or no straddles. Otherwise the operator has complex pairs (the cliff, 47 min) or real growth.
- **The cure.** With a horizontally uniform reference `T̄(z)`, `S̄(z)` (`TracerReferenceProfile`): (1) the force is the anomaly's (`ρ − ρ̄(z)` at the node's own height at the stage's `η`), integrated by the trapezoid rule, so it is linear and zero at rest over any bed; (2) along exactly the edges the force acts along, the reference's share of a tracer's jump is advected by the tangent `γ_a (z_b − z_a)` instead of the chord, `γ_a` the reference's mean gradient over the layer: `H ∂ρ′/∂t = Γ H w` with `w` the force's own adjoint; (3) what the tangents make in an element (the trapezoid rule's error along its edges) is taken back as a displacement of the element's stratification in the shape of its residual `g` about each column's own line in `z`, and the force leaves out that rule's error of the element's displacement `κ = Σ m H g ρ′/Σ m H g Γ` (the adjoint of that step; the regression of the anomaly on the stratification within the columns, so an anomaly uniform or linear in `z` has none); (4) every column's pressure is shifted so that its mean is the anomaly's first moment about the bed, the partner of the layers' rise through the reference with the free surface; (5) in an element with a thin column the subcell interfaces between its other columns are edges too: the tangent along them for the layers' shares of the barotropic flux, and a depth-mean force as its adjoint. Then, for the scheme linearised about rest with the layers' central fluxes, the force's work and the rate of `½ g Σ m H ρ′²/Γ` cancel exactly, with a depth mean in the transports or without, with the node's own stratification as the weight (no weight is singular), and every element keeps its inventories of T and S. That is a proof for this exchange, not for the model: the 2D Riemann fluxes, the splitting in time and a state that has left the reference are outside it (the doc's "Where the proof stops").
- **What an edge cannot do.** With single-valued fluxes between cells paired by level index, exact conservation, the skew structure and bounded weights exclude each other (it is the pair density's stiffness again). The element can: every edge the force acts along lies within one element.
- **Steps 4 and 5 came from the probes (2026-11-21).** With steps 1–3 the 30-vector spectra contracted, but the terrace's leading Ritz value rose to 1.0127 per half hour at 100 vectors (in the shore elements' bed layers and the barotropic fields), and the pseudo-energy of a random departure rose from 0.1477 of its start at 96 h to 0.1507 at 144 h. Part of that was the harness (the conserved volume, heat and salt each have a neutral direction, another state at rest, which Arnoldi approaches slowly and from both sides: the probes now take the map on the departures that change none of them; and the anomaly in the probe's norm is now about the reference at the layer's risen height). The rest was real: with a depth mean in the gate's transports the identity missed by 8 % of the work on stretched levels (3 % on uniform ones) and by 4 % more with a shore element. With the totals taken out, the terrace at 100 vectors: 1.0061 before steps 4–5 (e-folding 82 h), 0.99979 after (step 5 did it: 0.99980 without step 4).

### The step's bound after the check (2026-11-21, PR #259)

The check above found the first rule for the sloping levels (`ω Δt ≤ 1` with `ReferenceFields::sloping_level_rate`'s bound) setting the production step. It was slack twice over: the bound is 1.1–1.5 times the frequency, and the stages reach further along the imaginary axis than 1. The rule now (`Hydrostatic3D::compute_dt_ssp`, `element_steps_into`, `time_step_limits`, which take the `SspScheme`):

- **The scheme's reach.** `SspScheme::imaginary_axis_limit`: `√3` for SSP-RK3, `√(√160 − 8) = 2.156` for SSP-RK(4,3) (`|R(iy)|² = 1 − y⁴/24 + y⁶/144 + y⁸/2304`), tested on `y′ = iωy` through the stages. The step takes 0.85 of it (`Hydrostatic3D::SLOPING_LEVEL_MARGIN`): `ω Δt ≤ 1.47` and `1.83`.
- **The frequency, measured.** `ReferenceFields::sloping_level_frequency`: power iteration on `BᵀB` of the element's operator applied pair by pair, the element's displacement (the take-back) in it, in density (`e = 2(ρ̄_j − ρ̄_i) + (Γ_i + Γ_j)(z_j − z_i)` along each pair: the transpose of the force's displacement term). Stops at 0.03 % between iterations, 40 at most. Against the operator assembled from the kernels (`the_sloping_level_rate_bounds_the_pairs_frequency`, thermocline and smooth references, distorted elements, both metric forms): 0.996–1.000 of it, where the bound is 1.07–1.54 times it.
- **Which elements.** Those whose bound comes within a factor 1.5 of the step the other bounds allow (the bound does not count the take-back, which has put the frequency 3 % above it); the others report the bound's own step, which does not bind. On the cliff slices and the terrace fixture: the cliff's element only.
- **Shore elements keep the first rule** (`ω Δt ≤ 1` with the bound, 2–3 times the frequency): their flow along the subcell interfaces is the barotropic pass's, and the stages' reach is not the limit of that pairing. Not derived, not measured (Parked).
